## Where to Buy Equipment

\[Updated April 2026.\]

The most economic way of buying equipment is to buy the best value
pieces of kit separately. Boxed sets are mostly naff and include
unnecessary items. Separate pieces are available from various shops, but
for many items the **<a href="https://www.croquetengland.org.uk/shop/"
target="_blank">Croquet England Shop</a>** offers better prices than the
high-street. Contact the CqE shop on 01242 233 555 or email
<office@croquetengland.org.uk> . Note that being an
<a href="https://www.croquet.org.uk/?p=ca/JoinUs" target="_blank">U25
<strong>Premium</strong> Croquet England Member</a> (£10.50/£18.00)
gives you a **10%** member discount on many item in the CqE Shop, so if
buying a George Wood mallet (£265-374), it is cheaper to join and buy a
mallet with discount than buy a mallet on its own, etc. All you have to
do is persuade your college/JCR to buy into the idea! Do check that the
sum remains correct and exactly which items and how many of each are
discounted in your membership deal!

## Mallets

When buying mallets there are several factors to consider, as well as
price. (See <a href="https://oxfordcroquet.org/equip/mallets/"
target="_blank">Oxford Croquet</a> for more than you wanted to know):

- **Handle length**  
  The most common length is 36" (~91 cm), but if you are buying several
  for a college, it may be useful to include one that is slightly
  longer, for example 38" (96.5 cm), which may be more comfortable for
  taller players. Conversely, a shorter one can be useful, although this
  is less critical since players can always hold their hands slightly
  lower down a long shafted mallet.  
- **Handle material**  
  Although mallets with wooden shafts are sometimes cheaper than other
  types, **for general use in a college it IS worth spending a little
  extra to buy a mallet with a carbon fibre or metal shaft** which will
  not break easily if it receives rough treatment from inexperienced
  players. Having replacement handles/shafts fitted is expensive.  
- **Weight**  
  The typical weight is approximately 3lb (1360 g), since this often
  provides the best compromise for the demands of different strokes.
  While handle length is a common option on mallets you normally accept
  the provided weight unless you want a bespoke mallet.  
- **Head length**  
  Basic mallets often have approximately 9" long heads. However, where a
  longer head-length is an available option, it is well worth
  considering since longer heads are more 'stable' and more accurate to
  play with. For this reason the mallets used by elite players all tend
  to be about 11" long. Bear in mind also that longer length heads are
  easier to catch on the ground as the mallet is swung - for this reason
  **for college use it is not advisable to buy a mallet with a head that
  is longer than 11".** 10 - 11" is fine.  
- **Head width**  
  Narrower heads enable some shots to be played that might be hampered
  by a wider head, and most more expensive mallets tend to be quite
  narrow. However, for inexperienced players, such as **for general
  college use, it is advisable to have a mallet with a standard head**
  to reduce the likelihood of miss-hitting.

## **Hoops**

The ideal hoops for college are heavy metal hoops. Currently these would
include the [Croquet England Championship Croquet
Hoops](https://www.croquetengland.org.uk/product/croquet-england-championship-croquet-hoops/)
(£354) or if you have very deep pockets the <a
href="https://www.croquetengland.org.uk/product/ultra-championship-croquet-hoops-set-of-6/"
target="_blank">Ultra Championship Croquet Hoops</a> (£660!) If your
college buys solid metal hoops they should last many years. Tube and
wire hoops are poor substitutes but budgets can be limited. See
available hoops on the <a href="https://www.croquetengland.org.uk/shop/"
target="_blank">Croquet England Shop</a> site. If possible avoid hoops
which are 4" (10.1 cm) wide; the intended gape is 3.75" (~9.5 cm).
